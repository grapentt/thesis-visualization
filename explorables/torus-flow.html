Dimensionality reduction · topology of the target space

A flat square can hold the torus’s density, but not its flow

The data live on a torus embedded in ℝ³. The particle cloud below circulates around both of its holes while its distribution stays exactly p. Map the same particles onto the flat coordinate square and the dynamics only survive if opposite edges are glued, so particles jump. Remove the gluing, as any honest piece of ℝ² must, and either the distribution or the dynamics breaks.

A closed surface does not fit in the plane

Suppose a continuous injective map sent the torus into ℝ². The torus is locally a plane, so by invariance of domain the image would be open. It is also compact, hence closed. A nonempty set that is open and closed is all of ℝ², which is not compact. So every map to a 2D Euclidean target must tear the torus somewhere or fold parts of it onto each other. Here the tear is the pair of coloured circles, and the jumps in Fig. 2 are where you pay for it.

The current is a topological object

The flow carries a stationary current pv that is divergence-free but winds around both holes, with flux j/2π through every meridian. On a square with no-flux walls, integrate ∇·(pv) = 0 over the region left of any vertical cut: the flux through the cut must be zero. A circulating stationary state with net winding therefore cannot exist there. That is the “Laps” column for Fig. 3.

What a flat target can and cannot keep

A process on the square can always be tuned to have stationary law p, even an irreversible one whose current circulates in closed eddies inside the square. What is lost is the winding: the flux through every cut is zero, so no stationary state carries mass around the torus’s cycles, and lap rates and cycle transit times cannot be reproduced. If the reduction must reproduce dynamics, the target has to carry the topology, for example an embedding in ℝ³ or a latent space that is itself a product of circles S¹ × S¹.
