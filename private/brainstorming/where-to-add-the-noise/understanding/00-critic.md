## Headlines of every report

### r1-wdelta-era
From July to October 2026 the project tried to run score-based diffusion directly on the quotient shape manifold P(d,n)/E(d) with Diepeveen's wδ metric. That metric is the pullback of a flat feature space F(X)=((log r_ij), √δ log det G_X). It measures relative deformation, puts collisions and loss of affine rank infinitely far away, and splits exactly into a flat log-scale coordinate z times a curved shape factor. Using the tensor intrinsically left no closed-form forward kernel: every noising step was a stiff geodesic ODE solve, labels became separation-gradient surrogates (s_log), and the reverse sampler needed hundreds of exponentials per sample. A long chain of real bugs was found and fixed: the tensor transpose, the score sign, the τ=2 factor, an eigh-derivative NaN, TF32 matmuls, the clamp granularity, a forward/reverse clock mismatch, the covector-ISM divergence and an invented Jacobian factor. An early win (ALDP n=6, JS 0.117 vs 0.479) looked large. But the five-bead ALDP backbone is nearly planar, which is exactly the metric's infinite-distance rank barrier (worst condition ≈1.55×10⁶ vs 4.2×10³ with Cβ). Finiteness needed an exact scale/shape split plus a virtual phantom bead, and even then the distribution stayed wrong (α_L 20–50× over-populated, bonds 50–100× too broad) at ≈340–600× flat sampling cost. Once the flat baseline's own vp_mean bug was fixed (0.26→0.10; ScoreMD-native flat 0.0151 vs a 0.0114 floor), wδ's apparent advantage vanished. Sprint 10 (closed 2026-10-22) kept flat Cartesian as the default and demoted wδ to a restricted-domain reference. From 2026-10-23 the question became what the metric should do to the noise. By that criterion wδ at δ=1 is stiffer than flat on ALDP5 (κ 413 vs 192), yet the same features later worked as a cheap Euclidean embedding (Sprint 12).

### r2-stiffness-what-we-want
Era 2 began when Sprint 10 (#520, closed 2026-10-22) found no metric that beat flat diffusion, and PR #745 (theory notes 20–21, 2026-10-23/24) restarted from first principles: what should a stiffness-aware metric actually do for learning? It split flat diffusion's trouble into three problems, each tied to its own modelling choice. Uneven information loss belongs to the forward noise: flat noise erases ALDP5 bonds (width 0.025–0.030 Å) at a noise level about 20× smaller than torsions (0.2–0.7 Å), and the trusted schedule's floor (σ = 0.045 Å at t = 0.01) never resolves a bond. A badly scaled target belongs to the target and the input/output units: sizes and gains spread by √κ and κ. A misweighted loss belongs to the loss norm: up to κ per noise level, but only about 1.5× once integrated over levels. A thermal metric G ≈ βH = Σ⁻¹ fixes each problem through separately provable effects E1–E4. Note 21's pairing theorem shows that the covector, Riemannian and whitened scores learn the same map with the same loss value in their natural pairings. Only the whitened score with thermal inputs has a training curvature near 1 instead of κ² (on the ALDP5 window: 31 against 24,319 gradient-descent steps). Under flat noise the right ruler is the noise-level metric G_t. A constant rotation-equivariant metric cannot separate stretch from bend, so a stiffness metric must depend on the configuration (the spring metric cuts residual stiffness from 97–192 to 8–13). That makes it curved and makes metric-shaped noise only approximate, which led to pullbacks and embeddings in the next era. Sprint 11 confirmed the stiff-mode predictions on ALDP5 (bonds about 100× and angles 10–20× sooner; flat diffusion cannot get stiff and soft forces right at one noise floor) and the predicted limits (no torsion or rare-basin gain). About thirty loss- or target-only changes never improved generation. On BBA, removing stiffness from the whole molecule failed for measurable reasons explained in note 28, so on 2026-11-14 it was kept only for the stiff coordinates and the work moved to range and order.

### r3-embeddings-pullbacks-aldp5
In Sprint 11 (2026-10-23 to about 11-06, tracker #769) the project stopped integrating geodesics on configuration space and instead mapped molecules into a flat Euclidean feature space whose pullback metric G = DφᵀDφ measures every direction in its own thermal width. Diffusion then runs as ordinary Gaussian VP noise in the feature space (exact kernel, no geodesic ODE), and samples are projected back onto valid molecules. Notes 22–23 (2026-10-25) proved that an equal-dimension flat map can never reproduce the curved thermal metric. The forced error is at least Kρ²/12, which is small on ALDP5: a few percent per basin, heuristically 3–20% over the populated torsion landscape. A map into a higher-dimensional flat space can reproduce it exactly, and the spring-force and wδ metrics are already such pullbacks. On ALDP5 every stiffness-aware map (trajectory springs, wδ, learned warp, quantum springs, shared quantum map, invariant whitening, graph metrics) learned bonds and angles 10–100× sooner than the best flat model. Their extracted forces passed a stiff-direction Stein check and gave near-MD bonds and angles in Brownian dynamics, resolving the flat model's bonds-or-torsions dilemma. None improved torsions, rare αL populations, equilibrium sampling or transfer to unseen molecules (AC, BBA). Much of the gain came from simple global scaling: invariant whitening matched the nonlinear fits, and flat caught up with longer training. On BBA the benefit stayed local: springs had 52× better bonds in #895. In Sprint 12 springs and whitening failed for diagnosable reasons (wrong normalization scale, noise living off the redundant molecular image), which led to the range-and-order picture.

### r4-bba-scaling-learned-maps
Era 4 (2026-10-24 to 2026-11-14) tried to learn the stiffness-removing map from data and to scale it from the 5-bead ALDP5 to the 28-bead protein BBA. A coupling flow fitted to make 1 ps trajectory steps isotropic removed most local stiffness on ALDP5: local step condition number fell from 184 to 2.99, against a sampling floor of 1.55. Bonds and angles reached the data floor in about 1,000 instead of 100k–200k updates, but torsions were learned more slowly. The lag of the fitted steps turned out to be a dial: a 100 ps warp learned all of ALDP5 in 100 updates. That speed-up came from Gaussianizing the slow modes, not from stiffness removal (corrected the same day, 2026-11-05), and it cost late accuracy and rare-barrier fidelity. On BBA every stiffness-removing map improved bonds, but global shape (size, packing) did not improve. In the Sprint 12 first chunks, internal coordinates gave near-exact local geometry but molecules that were too large (Rg W1 0.521 Å against 0.072 Å for Cartesian), Cartesian had the right size but poor bonds (0.415 Å), and springs and whitening failed outright. Learned embeddings failed for measurable reasons: biased local targets (bond turning; BBA "1 ps" pairs are 200 ps chords), a mismatch between first-order fits and the nonlinear response, reversal by diffusion's single RMS normalization, and redundant ambient dimensions where noise leaves the molecule. Above all, equalizing every motion erases the fold in the same noise band as the local detail. PR #1025 diagnosed each failure and showed that what the network can read, and whether the analytic base is prior-matched in collective directions, matter more than noise shaping. This led to the 2026-11-14 "range and order" hypothesis: scale stiff coordinates to their thermal width and keep a few collective modes loud.

### r5-information-geometry-retention
Note 25 (2026-11-04) showed that the project's thermal metric, the inverse covariance of short trajectory steps, is a Fisher metric whose lag τ (or the spring soft scale γ = 1/(D₀τ)) is a dial. At small τ it is flat, at intermediate τ it is the within-basin thermal metric, and at large τ it is global whitening. Under noise shaped by it, modes are erased in order of relaxation time: half erasure at σ²/α² = 1/(1−e^{−2τ/t_k}). Measured on ALDP5: bonds and angles relax below 1 ps, φ libration in about 10 ps, ψ libration in 20–50 ps, the αR↔β hop in 100–200 ps and the αL hop in 5–20 ns. So the 1 ps metric weighs ψ about 19× too stiff. The note also identifies one matrix, the whitened posterior covariance Λ_t, that contains denoiser conditioning, equal erasure (E4), sampler stiffness and basin splitting. Each trajectory has only 37 independent αL visits, so no metric can fix that basin's weight better than to order one. Note 26 (2026-11-04) redefined the goal as retaining physical distinctions: I_t = Cov(E[u|X_t]). This separates information the noise destroys from information a finite predictor cannot read, and gives exact laws: a distribution width retains r², products retain r_i·r_j, and basin retention is Var(q)/(w(1−w)). The same-day experiments on 2026-11-05 tested this. Allocating training time to the bond band helped bonds but hurt angles and torsions. Learned retention maps learned faster early on, and all good maps converged later. The 100 ps warp learned torsions within 100 updates, but a γ sweep refuted synchronized erasure as the cause: the warp works by Gaussianizing the slow modes. The BBA work of 2026-11-11 to 11-18 then replaced E4's "erase everything together" with "range and order": every feature readable inside the trained noise range, global shape erased last. The existing retention video shows the theory on a toy, but none of the measured numbers, no continuous lag dial, no Λ_t, and none of the range-and-order sequel.

### r6-range-and-order
Range and order is the project's current account of what a noising chart must provide. A property can only be learned while it is readable: R_h(t) = Var(E[h|Y_t])/Var(h) can be measured from the data and the map without training, and in exact generation it equals the fraction of the property already decided at that noise level. So every property needs a readable window inside the trained levels (range), and large-scale shape must be erased last (order). On BBA, Cartesian noise has order without range: bond lengths are half erased at t≈0.0004, below t_min. Unit-spread internal and whitened charts have range without order: everything is erased between t≈0.18 and 0.35. Internal coordinates are even anti-ordered, because chain geometry is a high-degree product of torsion phases (R = Σ c_j r^j / Σ c_j). Appending the two largest collective modes unwhitened ('loud': mean squares 15.0 and 6.0, against ≈1 for every internal channel) restores the order, since mode 1 stays readable to t≈0.5. It also gives size a simple, near-Gaussian marginal and adds 3 constraint directions; their small-noise signal is split L²/(1+L²) against 1/(1+L²), so the quieter internal block is corrected towards the block. In the whitening-exponent family the size error fell 0.51→0.19→0.12→0.08 Å. Ordered internal coordinates keep internal-level local geometry and cut C3's size error about 4×: 0.117/0.091 vs 0.455/0.418 Å at 190,350 updates, and 0.111 vs 0.455 Å at the full 1,903,500. C3 never fixes its size with more training. The block cannot supply an excluded-volume wall (a range failure), global handedness, the kinematics for free, or sharp soft forces. Overlaps are handled by a late repulsion or by a joint rebuild with a hard core. A correction on 2026-11-16 showed that most of the loud-mode force-field compaction came from a wrong volume term, but every model still drifts compact over 100–300 ps.

### r7-current-frontier
Since the 2026-11-14 range-and-order decision, ordered internal coordinates (C3's 103 thermally scaled internal channels plus the two largest collective modes left loud, 109 features) have held up at the registered budget, on library code with three seeds and at the full 1,903,500-update schedule: internal-level local geometry (bond W1 about 0.0003-0.0004 A, 17-120x better than every Cartesian-family model and ScoreMD), size error 0.09-0.12 A (C3 0.42-0.47 A, best Cartesian 0.07-0.08 A), populations tied with the best (ScoreMD-paper PMF 0.097 vs data floor 0.080 at 100,000 molecules). Theory note 30 (2026-11-18) explains why: internal coordinates erase long-range geometry before the torsions that build it (anti-order, Proposition 1: a product of j torsion phases keeps r^j of its readability), while the redundant loud block survives the most noise and adds 3K-3 constraint directions whose predictable noise is split L^2/(1+L^2) : 1/(1+L^2), so the quieter internal block is corrected toward the block's size (Proposition 2); two Rouse-like modes carry 96% of a Gaussian chain's size response and are the only modes louder than a torsion. The force-field work of the week found that three quarters of the loud-mode compaction was a wrong volume term in the score-to-force conversion (rotation-invariant formula instead of the immersion term; 100 ps size error 0.98 -> 0.24 A), that the excluded-volume wall cannot be learned from the score at any trained level (contacts are a range failure, half-erased at t about 0.013), and that a wall fitted against simulations needs a weak size term (-0.16 kT per A of Rg) to avoid swelling, after which overlaps go to 0% with size at the panel floor. Read-out repairs that need no retraining - the joint least-squares rebuild from all 109 numbers with a 3.6 A hard core (0% overlaps, Rg W1 0.083/0.070 A) and the fitted wall+size correction - transfer across seeds and to the full schedule. What remains: every loud-mode force field still compacts between 100 and 300 ps (mean Rg -0.21 to -0.41 A), the model itself still overlaps 12-20% of molecules, a 0.04-0.06 handedness deficit in the 9.5-11 A bin was traced to the relative twist of the chain's two halves, and no simulation can yet show equilibrium populations of a microsecond folder. Optimism rests on a large, reproducible gain, derived mechanisms that predicted both the optimal block size and the reverse-path takeover time (R^2 >= 0.5 first at t = 0.13, predicted 0.26-0.1), and on the fact that each remaining defect sits exactly where the theory says a two-vector block cannot help.

### r8-systems-data-eval
The evidence rests on two systems whose datasets changed during the work. ALDP5 is five backbone beads of alanine dipeptide (9 internal coordinates). Evaluation moved from Ramachandran JS (Sprint 1) to a ratified ALDP5 PMF/JS/bond contract (2026-10-08), then a pre-declared protocol in thermal widths (2026-10-26), then the Sprint 12 physical readout (bond/angle/Rg W1, contacts, population JS, clashes) and the paper-definition PMF (PR #1138). ### g1-origins-and-consistency
The thesis was pitched (exposé June 2026, built 2026-07-02; supervisor decks of 2026-07-31 and 2026-08-03) as the empty cell between ScoreMD and Diepeveen's wδ shape manifold. ScoreMD is flat, Fokker–Planck-consistent diffusion whose small-t energy also serves as a force field; wδ gives geometry but no generator. The plan was a Riemannian diffusion model plus flow matching with a Laplace–Beltrami FP regulariser on (M, g_wδ), judged by iid- and simulation-PMF on BBA. Conservative wδ heads never produced physical samples (Sprints 2 and 6) and the only flow-matching pilot lost about 3× (JS 0.620 vs 0.199). On 2026-10-06 the milestones were re-ordered, which pushed conservative modelling, manifold FP and forces to M3/M4 and RFM to M5; none of these ever ran as specified, and Sprint 10 (closed 2026-10-22) ended the intrinsic-manifold route. ScoreMD's consistency problem is a read-out dilemma. Forces read at t=1e-5 sample well but simulate a wrong landscape (BBA simulation PMF 5.387). Forces read at t>0 (Two For One) are stable but blur stiff bonds (simulated C–N bond 23.51× worse on ALDP). FP+MoE repairs simulation (0.254) but iid PMF rises from 0.034 to 0.234. The stiffness theory explains both horns. A force read at level t is the force of the blurred density, so each mode's restoring strength scales by its readable fraction r(t) and its simulated width grows by 1/√r. At the same time one small noise level carries soft-force information only ∝σ². On ALDP5 (#768, 2026-10-27), metric-shaped coordinates resolved the split from a single read-out level without FP; the docs call this explicitly "a parallel, not a head-to-head". On BBA the internal-coordinate force fields are the best measured: bonds stay at the panel floor and size within 0.8–1.2 Å after 50–100 ps, against 1.9 Å for the best Cartesian model and 3.2–3.5 Å for the unregularised native ScoreMD arm. A wrong volume term caused three quarters of the loud-mode compaction (100 ps size error 0.98 → 0.24 Å). Still, every force field keeps compacting through 300–500 ps and none has learned an excluded-volume wall. "Change the coordinates" is the working force-field strategy. FP consistency stays a reserved, unimplemented remedy, and the paper-protocol simulation PMF (PR #1138) is still unmeasured.

### g2-straightening-and-wdelta-arc
Diepeveen 2024 offers two pieces of evidence, and the project's exposé (2026-07-02) built its case on them. First, on a 102-frame closed→open adenylate kinase transition, a single "wδ-geodesic" between the end frames stays within the 3.85 Å Cα spacing for most residues. Its RMSD peaks near 2.5 Å at mid-path (read from figure). Second, PCA of the tangent vectors at the wδ-barycentre reconstructs that transition from one component (about 85% of variance, about 1 Å error); nsp13 needs 7 components. Precisely, the "wδ-geodesic" is not a geodesic but a separation interpolation, and by an exact identity it is the realizable molecule nearest to a straight line through log pair distances. The Sprint 10 re-examination (2026-10-19, issues #513 and #522) found that the AK input is a DIMS directed transition and that no theorem links wδ to MD paths. A plain aligned Cartesian chord follows the frames better (1.76–1.78 vs 2.03–2.05 Å on AK; 0.224 vs 0.247 Å on ALDP5); wδ's only robust advantage is preserving short distances (adjacent-Cα error 0.077 vs 0.144 Å). My own checks (unofficial) show that plain Cartesian PCA reproduces the rank-1 result on the same AK data (0.8934 variance, 0.747 Å; ProteinAtlas's wδ reproduction: 0.8927, 0.748 Å) and the rank-7 result on nsp13, so the compression comes from the directed data, not from the metric. The "straightened coordinates make the score linear" intuition did come true later, but through Gaussianization by a learned flow: refitted on 100 ps steps, the warp made ALDP5 nearly Gaussian (KL 0.14 vs 0.96) and learned the torsions in 100 updates. That is exactly Diepeveen 2025's construction, including its unimodal limit, which shows up as a surplus at the rare αL barrier. As a BBA feature map at the registered budget, wδ has the best size error, zero severe clashes and a PMF better than ScoreMD, but bonds 86× worse than ordered internal coordinates and force fields with 17–18° angle errors. A first-order readable-window screen (my own) explains this mirror-image profile: closest contacts stay readable up to t½ ≈ 0.12 in wδ versus 0.013 in internal coordinates, while bonds are erased right at the floor (t½ ≈ 0.014).

### g3-literature-positioning
The two halves of the project's story correspond to two literatures that push the data's variance spectrum in opposite directions. Ahamed et al. 2026, EDM input scaling, Everaert's covariance realignment, Falck's EqualSNR and Wang & Pehlevan's spectral bias say: flatten it. Otherwise narrow, stiff directions are learned slowly, weighted weakly by the loss, and erased below the trained noise range. Rissanen, Wang & Vastola, Biroli et al., Li & Chen, Sclocchi et al., Pan et al., Dieleman and EigenFold say: a steep spectrum produces a coarse-to-fine order in which high-variance structure is decided first. Full whitening is ideal for the first view and destroys the second. Cartesian coordinates are the reverse. The project's current map makes the split explicitly: internal coordinates at thermal width give range, and two loud collective modes give order. Note 21's aim of synchronized information loss (2026-10-24) became the diagnosed cause of lost order (note 27, 2026-11-12); this was the pivotal correction. Every ingredient has precedent: transform-train-invert (Ahamed), pullback noising (R-DM), ordered protein noising (EigenFold, Chroma), per-component schedules (GUD), and appended global latents with leading schedules (semantic-first diffusion and latent forcing in images). What remains new is the measured range/order decomposition for molecules, the training-free readability probe on nonlinear molecular observables, anti-order and the constraint split in a redundant chart, and the immersion-volume force read-out. Two thesis gaps: Ahamed (direct prior art for note 21's curvature theorem and the learned warp) is cited nowhere in docs/, and Related_Work.md and the REFERENCES R-DM row still carry wδ-era claims, some of which the 2026-10-19 library note itself refuted.

### g4-number-canon
